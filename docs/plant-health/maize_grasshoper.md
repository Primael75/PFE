# Grasshoppers on maize

> This is a **suspicion** based on a photo. It must be **confirmed by an agricultural technician** before any decision.

> **Sheet at pest-group level**: the photo dataset does not give the grasshopper species. This sheet therefore names no species and suggests no specific treatment.

## In short

Grasshoppers eat the leaves of crops. Maize is one of the crops they prefer [S1]. In Ghana, at least one grasshopper species, *Zonocerus variegatus*, is a major agricultural pest [S2].

## Symptoms

- **Eaten leaves**, chewed by adult grasshoppers and by wingless young ones [S1, S2].
- **Insects visible** on the plants or in nearby grass.

## Importance

- Grasshoppers that settle in a field often stay there and can eat a large share of the crop [S1].
- Grasshoppers that move **in swarms** destroy everything in their path. A single farmer can rarely fight them: the agricultural services must be alerted [S1].

## Confirmation

- Only a **technician** can confirm the species and the extent of the attack. Showing a captured insect helps identify it.

## What to do

1. **Have it confirmed** by an agricultural technician; in case of a swarm, **alert the agricultural services immediately** [S1].
2. **Destroy the eggs**: females lay in the soil, in shallow holes. Find these places, dig up the egg pods with a hoe and leave them to dry in the sun or give them to poultry [S1].
3. **Act with the neighbours**: if neighbouring fields do not do the same, the grasshoppers come back [S1].
4. **Let poultry roam** (ducks, guinea fowl, chickens): they eat grasshoppers [S1].
5. **Cover the soil and weed**: a covered soil discourages egg laying [S1].
6. **Chemical treatment**: the source considers it more costly and less safe than the methods above [S1]. No product or dose is given here: the technician decides.

## What the agent must not do

- Name a grasshopper species.
- Suggest an insecticide, a dose or a treatment schedule.
