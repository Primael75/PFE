# Cassava mosaic disease

> This is a **suspicion** based on a photo. It must be **confirmed by an agricultural technician** before any decision.

## In short

A disease caused by a virus [S1]. It spreads mainly through **cuttings taken from diseased plants**, and also through the **whitefly** (*Bemisia tabaci*), which carries the virus from a diseased plant to a healthy one [S1]. Because cassava is grown from cuttings, the disease can build up from one crop to the next [S1].

## Symptoms

- **Leaves** yellow or almost white, deformed, with smaller leaflets [S1].
- **Yellow and green patches** that do not follow the veins [S2].
- **Stunted plants**; severely affected plants give few or no roots [S1].

## Importance

- Losses can reach 90 % in severe cases [S1].
- Across sub-Saharan Africa, the disease is estimated to destroy 15 to 24 % of the cassava crop each year [S1].

## Confirmation

- The cassava green mite also yellows the leaves, but it causes a "candle stick" on the shoots, which mosaic does not [S2].
- Only a **technician** can confirm the diagnosis.

## What to do

1. **Have the diagnosis confirmed** by an agricultural technician.
2. **Never replant cuttings from diseased plants**: take cuttings only from plants without symptoms, and choose healthy stems at harvest for selling, exchanging or replanting [S1].
3. **Resistant or tolerant varieties** show much milder symptoms: ask the technician which ones are available [S1].
4. **Pull out diseased plants** shortly after sprouting, when the disease is not spreading fast [S1].
5. **Whitefly control** is possible but difficult and costly, because whiteflies are everywhere [S1].
6. **No product cures a plant** infected by the virus: our source gives no chemical treatment [S1].

## What the agent must not do

- Present the diagnosis as certain.
- Suggest a product that would cure the plant, a dose or a treatment schedule.
- Advise replanting cuttings from a diseased field.
