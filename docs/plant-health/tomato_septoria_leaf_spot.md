# Tomato septoria leaf spot

> This is a **suspicion** based on a photo. It must be **confirmed by an agricultural technician** before any treatment decision.

## In short

A leaf disease caused by a fungus, *Septoria lycopersici* [S1]. It is one of the main fungal diseases of tomato recorded in Ghana [S2]. In rainy weather it can quickly destroy the foliage [S1].

## Symptoms

- **Small, round, dark spots** on the leaves, up to about 3 mm across [S1].
- **Grey-white centre**: many spots end up with a grey-white centre [S1].
- **Tiny black dots** in the centre of the spots: these are the fruiting bodies of the fungus [S1].
- **Progression**: the disease starts on the lower leaves, then reaches the new leaves [S1].

## Confirmation

- These spots can look like those of bacterial spot or early blight [S1].
- Only a **technician** can confirm the diagnosis.

## What to do

1. **Have the diagnosis confirmed** by an agricultural technician.
2. **Rotation**: grow something other than tomato on the plot in the following years [S1].
3. **Clean the plot**: remove as much crop debris as possible, do not compost it, bury the rest, and remove weeds of the tomato family during the rotation [S1].
4. **Help the foliage dry**: stake and prune the plants [S1].
5. **Water at the foot** of the plants, not by sprinkling the leaves [S1].
6. **Use healthy transplants** [S1].
7. **Varieties**: no resistant variety is reported [S1].
8. **Chemical treatment**: no product or dose is given here. The technician decides whether to treat.

## What the agent must not do

- Present the diagnosis as certain.
- Give a fungicide, a dose or a treatment schedule.
