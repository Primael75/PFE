# Fall armyworm

> This is a **suspicion** based on a photo. It must be **confirmed by an agricultural technician** before any decision.

## In short

A pest: the caterpillar of a moth, *Spodoptera frugiperda*, native to the Americas. Its first outbreaks in Africa were recorded in early 2016, in Nigeria, Benin, Togo and Sao Tome [S1]. It has almost 100 recorded host plants and prefers grasses: maize, millet, sorghum, rice, sugarcane [S1]. Severe outbreaks usually come at the start of the rainy season, especially after a long drought [S1].

## Symptoms

- **Young plants**: large caterpillars can cut the stem at its base [S1].
- **During growth**: leaves eaten down to their veins, whorls full of holes and filled with caterpillar droppings [S1].
- **On older plants**: caterpillars feed on the tassels and bore into the cobs [S1].

## Confirmation

- Older caterpillars can be confused with other caterpillars, such as the beet armyworm [S1].
- Only a **technician** can confirm the identification. Showing a caterpillar helps.

## What to do

1. **Have it confirmed** by an agricultural technician.
2. **Check the field regularly**: farmer field schools teach weekly monitoring [S2].
3. **Crush egg masses and caterpillars by hand**, visiting the field twice a week [S2]. In Benin, farmers in field schools fed the collected caterpillars to chickens [S2].
4. **Protect natural enemies**: ground beetles, earwigs, ladybirds, spiders, ants, birds and bats eat the caterpillars, and small parasitic wasps attack eggs and caterpillars [S2].
5. **Push-pull**: maize intercropped with desmodium and bordered by Napier grass was effective in demonstration plots [S2].
6. **Local remedies** (ash, sand or soil in the whorl, plant extracts): reported as promising by farmers, but not scientifically confirmed [S2]. Ask the technician before relying on them.
7. **Chemical treatment**: insecticides that act on contact often fail, because young caterpillars hide inside the plant, and overuse has made the pest resistant [S1]. Groups that used pesticides also reported harm to bees and ladybirds [S2]. No product or dose is given here: the technician decides.

## What the agent must not do

- Present the identification as certain.
- Suggest an insecticide, a dose or a treatment schedule.
- Present a home-made remedy as proven.
