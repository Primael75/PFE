# Tomato verticillium wilt

> This is a **suspicion** based on a photo. It must be **confirmed by an agricultural technician** before any decision.

## In short

A disease caused by a fungus of the genus *Verticillium*, common in soil. It enters through the roots, then spreads through the plant's vessels [S1]. Tomato and potato are preferred hosts [S1].

## Symptoms

- **Lower leaves**: they turn yellow first, dry out, turn brown and die [S1].
- **On older leaves**: V-shaped yellow areas starting from the leaf edge [S2].
- **Wilting and stunted growth**, leaves curling inwards [S1].
- **In a cut stem**: the vessels are brown [S1].
- **Consequences**: young plants can die; older plants often survive but yield less [S1, S2].

## Confirmation

- Verticillium wilt is very hard to tell apart from fusarium wilt. A reliable identification may need a laboratory analysis [S2, S3].
- Fusarium wilt is rather favoured by warm soils, verticillium wilt by cooler soils [S3].
- Only a **technician** can confirm the diagnosis.

## What to do

1. **Have the diagnosis confirmed** by an agricultural technician.
2. **Rotation**: do not replant tomato or potato in contaminated soil, except resistant varieties [S1].
3. **Resistant varieties**: they are usually marked with the letter "V" [S1, S3].
4. **Healthy transplants** at planting [S1].
5. **Weeds**: remove weeds of the tomato family (nightshades), which host the fungus [S1].
6. **Debris**: remove and destroy diseased plants, do not compost them [S1].
7. **Chemical treatment**: none is recommended by our sources [S1].

## What the agent must not do

- Present the diagnosis as certain, especially against fusarium wilt.
- Suggest a product or a dose.
