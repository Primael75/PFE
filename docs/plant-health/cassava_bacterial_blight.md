# Cassava bacterial blight

> This is a **suspicion** based on a photo. It must be **confirmed by an agricultural technician** before any decision.

## In short

A disease caused by a bacterium, *Xanthomonas phaseoli* pv. *manihotis*. It moves through the plant's vessels and can strongly affect growth and production [S2]. It is found in most cassava-growing countries [S1].

## Symptoms

- **On leaves**: dark angular spots, first bounded by the veins, with a creamy ooze. The spots grow, merge and form large rotten areas, especially at the leaf tips [S1].
- **Wilting**: infected leaves wilt, die and fall [S1].
- **In stems**: the bacteria move from the leaves into the stems and block the flow of water [S1].
- **"Candle" shoots**: leaves drop from the stem, the shoot tip turns black, and new leaves grow lower on the stem [S1].

## How it spreads

- By wind and rain [S1].
- Through **cuttings** taken from infected plants [S1].
- Through **contaminated tools** [S1].

## Confirmation

- Another bacterium, *Xanthomonas cassavae*, causes leaf symptoms similar to bacterial blight, but with a limited effect on the plant and the crop [S2].
- Only a **technician** can confirm the diagnosis.

## What to do

1. **Have the diagnosis confirmed** by an agricultural technician.
2. **Take cuttings only from healthy fields** [S1].
3. **Do not plant a new plot next to an old one** [S1].
4. **Rotate crops** for one to two years [S1].
5. **Clean tools** after use [S1].
6. **Collect and burn crop debris** after harvest [S1].
7. **Resistant varieties** exist: ask the technician which ones are available locally [S1].
8. **Chemical treatment**: none is recommended by our source [S1].

## What the agent must not do

- Present the diagnosis as certain.
- Suggest a product, a dose or a treatment schedule.
