# Cashew leaf miner

> This is a **suspicion** based on a photo. It must be **confirmed by an agricultural technician** before any decision.

## In short

A pest: the caterpillar of a very small moth digs tunnels ("mines") inside the young leaves of the cashew tree [S2, S3]. The species on the training photos, taken in Ghana, is not confirmed. In Benin, the species is *Eteoryctis gemoniella*, described as one of the most destructive cashew pests in the country [S1]. Since the photo alone cannot tell the species, this sheet names none.

## Symptoms

- **On young, tender leaves**: winding tunnels dug under the skin of the upper side of the leaf [S3].
- **Blisters**: the mined area swells and forms greyish-white blistered patches [S3].
- **On older leaves**: the mined areas dry out and leave large holes [S3].
- **Inside the mines**: small caterpillars, dull white to reddish brown [S2].
- **Adult**: a tiny silvery-grey moth [S2].

## Importance

- By reducing the useful surface of young leaves, the leaf miner lowers the tree's photosynthesis, and therefore its yield [S1].
- In northern Benin, attacks peaked in July-August and again in November, with no attack from January to June [S1].

## Confirmation

- Only a **technician** can confirm that it is a leaf miner and identify the species.

## What to do

1. **Have it confirmed** by an agricultural technician.
2. **Collect and destroy the attacked parts** [S3].
3. **Protect natural enemies**: small parasitic wasps of the leaf miner were found in Benin orchards [S1]. In India, natural enemies reduce populations of this type of pest by up to 50 % [S4].
4. **Treatment**: no product or dose is given here. The technician decides whether to treat.

## What the agent must not do

- Name a leaf miner species as certain.
- Suggest an insecticide, a dose or a treatment schedule.
