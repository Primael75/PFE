# Maize streak

> This is a **suspicion** based on a photo. It must be **confirmed by an agricultural technician** before any decision.

## In short

A disease caused by a virus, spread by **leafhoppers** (small insects of the genus *Cicadulina*) that feed on maize leaves [S1, S2]. The virus does not pass through the seed [S2]. Infected leafhoppers carry it from wild grasses, where it causes no symptoms, to the maize [S2].

## Symptoms

- **Yellow streaks** running along the veins of the leaves [S1, S2].
- **Timing**: symptoms appear 10 to 14 days after infection, mostly on young leaves [S2].
- **Young plants** (under six weeks) are the most affected; a plant attacked at the 4-5 leaf stage can be severely stunted [S1].
- **Cobs**: stunted plants give small cobs [S2].

## Importance

- From a few diseased plants per field to the loss of the whole harvest [S1, S2].
- Infection about three weeks after emergence can cause severe losses [S2].

## Confirmation

- Only a **technician** can confirm the diagnosis.

## What to do

1. **Have the diagnosis confirmed** by an agricultural technician.
2. **Certified seed** and **sowing at the start of the rains** [S1].
3. **Sow a large area at once**, which makes the crop less exposed to leafhoppers [S1].
4. **Inspect the field often while the maize is young** [S1].
5. **Pull out diseased plants** as soon as symptoms appear, and remove them from the field so that leafhoppers do not spread the disease [S1].
6. **Keep the field free of grasses** and remove cereal residues, which harbour the leafhoppers and the virus [S1, S2].
7. **Resistant varieties** exist: ask the technician which ones are available [S2].
8. **No product cures a plant** infected by the virus. Insecticide seed treatments against the leafhopper exist, but their cost and health risks hold many small farmers back [S2]. No product or dose is given here: the technician decides.

## What the agent must not do

- Present the diagnosis as certain.
- Suggest a product that would cure the plant, an insecticide, a dose or a treatment schedule.
