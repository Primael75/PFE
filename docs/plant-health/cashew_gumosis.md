# Cashew gummosis

> This is a **suspicion** based on a photo. It must be **confirmed by an agricultural technician** before any treatment decision.

## In short

A disease caused by fungi of the genus *Lasiodiplodia*. It attacks the trunk and branches of the cashew tree and can kill the tree [S1, S2]. The reference review considers it the most widespread and most damaging cashew disease [S1]. In West Africa, three *Lasiodiplodia* species were found responsible in Guinea-Bissau [S3].

## Symptoms

- **On the trunk and branches**: black cankers, open or closed. Over time they crack and ooze gum [S2].
- **Gum**: a brownish flow, in variable amounts [S2].
- **First signs**: cankers without gum flow [S2].
- **Decline**: twigs dry out, leaves fall, foliage turns yellow, then part or all of the tree dries out, sometimes until it dies [S1, S2].

## Importance

- In Nigeria, gummosis caused the loss of 40 to 50 % of the harvest [S1].
- In Burkina Faso, its incidence ranged from 64 % to 100 % depending on the province [S2].

## Confirmation

- Several fungi are isolated from trees with these symptoms: in Burkina Faso, *Lasiodiplodia* made up half of the fungi found, alongside *Pestalotia*, *Fusarium* and others [S2].
- Only a **technician** can confirm the diagnosis in the orchard. A reliable identification of the agent needs a laboratory analysis [S1, S2].

## What to do

1. **Have the diagnosis confirmed** by an agricultural technician.
2. **Disinfect pruning and grafting tools** and **avoid wounding the trees**: wounds and unclean tools are entry points for the fungus [S2].
3. **Avoid planting too densely**: trees that are too close together help the disease spread [S1].
4. **Treatment**: our sources recommend no specific treatment. The technician decides what to do.

## What the agent must not do

- Present the diagnosis as certain.
- Suggest a product, a dose or a trunk treatment method.
- Announce from a photo that a tree will die or must be cut down.
